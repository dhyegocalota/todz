# 0123. A refresh token retried within the leeway gets a fresh pair, and the cap holds a refresh every five minutes

- Status: Accepted (the loop's call)
- Date: 2026-11-27
- Deciders: Claude (the build loop's call, under the owner's report on 2026-11-27 that his Claude connector asked him to sign in again a few hours after he approved it)
- Inspired by: [0109](0109-mcp-clients-connect-with-oauth-2-1-pkce-and-dynamic-registration-and-a-grant-is-never-more-than-the-equivalent-key.md), [the feature](../features/oauth-refresh-grant/spec.md)

The loop's call: a refresh token spent less than 30 seconds ago, presented again by the client its grant belongs to, is honoured with a new pair on the same grant instead of answering `invalid_grant`, because an MCP client reads `invalid_grant` on a refresh as a revoked grant, drops its tokens and sends the owner back to sign in, so a retried or raced refresh (Claude refreshes from parallel requests) disconnected the app as surely as a theft. The leeway counts from the first spend and a retry never moves it; a spent token presented later still deletes the whole grant, and another client's token is still refused. A grant now keeps up to 10,000 unexpired refresh tokens, a refresh every five minutes for the 30 days a refresh token lives, where 1,000 was 1.4 times an hourly client and a client refreshing early and racing itself crossed it within three weeks; it is still revoked at the cap rather than forgetting a spent token. Answering the same pair again was the alternative, and it needs the token secrets stored, which ADR 0109 forbids; keeping a spent token valid until its successor is used covers a slower retry and needs a parent column per token. The accepted risk is the one ADR 0109 already names: a theft within 30 seconds of the real client's refresh is not detected. This amends ADR 0109 §8.
