# 0063. The day email is claimed once a day per owner from a quarter-hour cron

- Date: 2026-11-25
- Deciders: Claude (the build loop's call, pending the owner); Dhyego Calota (owner) chose idea 9 from the loop's `proactive-day` list: "at a time the owner picks (default 07:00 in their time zone), a short 'Your day' mail with Today, through `transactional-email`"; every item opt-in, off by default, a Settings toggle, no push notifications
- Inspired by: [ADR 0052](0052-the-waitlist-digest-goes-to-the-admin-address-once-a-day-from-a-cron.md) (whose first production run is still unconfirmed, so this one leaves a record of every send), [ADR 0053](0053-agents-mention-today-s-tasks-in-next-when-the-owner-turns-it-on.md) (an owner flag on the server written by a partial `PATCH /owner`), [ADR 0055](0055-the-day-closes-once-a-day-in-the-menu-bar-popover-at-the-owner-s-time.md) (once a day, on time or on the first chance after), [ADR 0018](0018-the-mcp-server-reads-today-in-the-owner-s-time-zone.md), [ADR 0036](0036-the-menu-bar-icon-counts-today-s-open-tasks-behind-a-setting.md), [ADR 0042](0042-emails-are-react-email-templates-rendered-at-build-time-into-a-generated-module.md), the plan and security review of 2026-11-25 (R1–R18 in the feature's `plan.md`)

## Context

The owner wants a short mail with Today at a time he picks in his own time zone. Cloudflare cron triggers are fixed UTC expressions, owners live in many zones, and some zones sit on half or quarter hours (India +05:30, Nepal +05:45), so no single daily cron reaches everyone at 07:00. The server already holds each owner's time zone (ADR 0018, refreshed at every sign-in and owner confirmation) and his sign-in address (the `email` identity's subject, ADR 0040). The waitlist digest (ADR 0052) runs once a day with no record, and nobody can tell today whether its first production run happened.

## Decision

1. **Two owner settings on the server, off by default.** `owners.day_email INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (day_email IN (0, 1))` and `owners.day_email_minutes INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 420 CHECK (day_email_minutes BETWEEN 0 AND 1439)` (07:00) in migration 0008. `GET /owner`, `PATCH /owner` and the sign-in answer carry `day_email` and `day_email_minutes`; the PATCH stays partial (every field optional, at least one). The server owns them because the server sends the mail.
2. **The recipient is the owner's sign-in address**, the subject of his `email` identity in `owner_identities`. Only an owner with exactly one `email` identity is due (`HAVING count(*) = 1`): none (the fake provider of development and tests) or two make the address ambiguous, so no mail, whatever the flag says. No other address can be set.
3. **A cron every fifteen minutes** (`*/15 * * * *`) beside the digest's. `runScheduled` moves out of the digest into a `scheduled` module whose record maps each cron to its run, so the digest keeps `0 11 * * *` and its behaviour.
4. **Due, then claimed once.** On each tick, for every owner with the flag on and an address, the owner's local date and minute come from his time zone through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with `hourCycle: 'h23'` (so midnight is `00`, never `24`); an owner whose stored zone no longer resolves is skipped without stopping the run. On a spring-forward day the skipped minutes simply never occur, so a chosen 02:30 in a zone that jumps from 02:00 to 03:00 is due at 03:00. The mail is due when the local minute is at or after the chosen minute and less than 180 minutes after it on the same local date (a catch-up window for a missed tick or an outage; past it, that day is skipped, so turning the mail on at 15:00 for 07:00 starts tomorrow, and a chosen 23:00 never spills into the next day). A due owner is claimed by `INSERT INTO day_emails (owner_id, day, claimed_at) … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` on the primary key `(owner_id, day)`; only the run whose insert changed a row sends. Two overlapping ticks never send twice, and a DST day that repeats an hour sends once.
5. **Every send leaves a record.** The claimed row gains `sent_at` after the send, or `failed_at` and a `failure` kind (`read_failed` when a project could not be read, `too_many_projects` when the owner has more projects than the cap of §6, `send_failed` when the binding threw). Nothing retries: a failed day stays failed. After trying every due owner, a run with any failure rejects with the count of failures only, so the dashboard marks the invocation failed without naming anyone. Rows of `day_emails` and `day_email_replies` older than 30 days, and expired reply addresses, are deleted on each tick. Workers Logs is turned on with invocation logs off (`observability: { enabled: true, logs: { invocation_logs: false } }`), because an invocation log of an `email` event records the envelope's `mailFrom` and `rcptTo`; each run's one summary line (`{event, due, sent, failed}`, never an address or a title) is kept.
6. **One owner at a time, at most 50 sends per tick, at most 100 projects per owner.** Due owners are handled sequentially, each inside its own `try`/`catch`, so one owner's failure never stops another's mail. Due owners past 50 wait for the next tick, still inside their window. An owner with more than 100 projects is recorded `too_many_projects` without reading any. Both caps bound one invocation's subrequests and live in `libs/types`; they are revisited when owners grow.
7. **"Today" is every workspace's.** The mail lists the open tasks due today or overdue (ADR 0036's Today, overdue included) across every workspace and project of the owner, in the directory's order: workspaces by creation, the Inbox first then projects by creation, tasks in each project's manual order, each line `T-12 Title · Project` with `· overdue` when overdue, and `Workspace / Project` when the owner has more than one workspace. At most 20 lines, then "and N more."; the subject counts all of them ("Your day: 3 due"). An empty Today still sends ("Your day: nothing due"), because silence would hide a broken cron (ADR 0052 §3) and the reply still adds a task. A project whose read fails fails the whole mail (`read_failed`): a partial Today would lie.
8. **The mail** is a React Email template on the shared layout, rendered at build time (ADR 0042) in two variants (with tasks, empty), from `noreply@todz.app`, its reply-to a fresh per-send reply address ([ADR 0064](0064-a-reply-to-the-day-email-adds-one-task-only-from-the-owner-s-own-authenticated-address.md)). It has no button: a `todz://` link is blocked by most mail clients and the web has no app. It carries `Auto-Submitted: auto-generated` (RFC 3834) through the binding's `headers`, so well-behaved responders do not answer it. `EmailMessage` gains a required `replyTo`; `filledEmailOf` defaults it to `hello@todz.app`, so every other mail keeps today's reply-to.
9. **The app owns the controls.** The Settings window gains "Email me my day" with a time and a switch, read from `GET /owner` and written by a partial `PATCH /owner`, disabled offline, signed out and while a save is in flight, like the agent nudge (ADR 0053 §6). Turning it off also deletes the owner's live reply addresses (ADR 0064 §1).
10. **A scheduled owner reaches projects through the directory.** ADR 0013 §2 opens a project only for a credential the directory has just resolved. The cron has no credential, so the candidates query is the only producer of a branded `ScheduledOwner` (kept in a `WeakSet`, like `SessionOwner`), and the routing module lists that owner's workspaces and projects itself and opens only those. No other module turns an owner id into a project stub.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Pros | Cons | Why not |
|--------|------|------|---------|
| `*/15` cron with a per-owner per-day claim in D1 (chosen) | reaches every zone, including quarter-hour ones; idempotent; a record of every send | 96 invocations a day, most doing one D1 read | the reads are cheap and the record answers "did it run?" |
| Hourly cron | fewer invocations | misses :30 and :45 zones by up to 45 minutes; a chosen 07:10 lands at 08:00 | too coarse for a time the owner picks |
| One Durable Object alarm per owner | exact to the minute | a new class, an alarm to re-arm on every change of time or zone, and nothing to list "who is due" | more moving parts than the mail is worth |
| Cloudflare Queues for the sends | retries, back-pressure | a new product, a consumer, a dead-letter path | not needed below the 50-per-tick cap |
| The app sends the mail at its own time | no server schedule | needs a Mac awake at 07:00; mail from the app's network | the server already sends mail |
| Today of the app's active workspace only | matches the menu bar count | the server has no "active workspace"; the person, not a window, reads the mail | every workspace, labelled |
| Skip empty days | quieter | silence hides a broken cron | ADR 0052's reasoning |
| Retry a failed send on the next tick | fewer lost days | a state machine and a risk of double mail after a timeout that did send | one record, no retry, the failure is visible |

## Consequences

- Migration 0008 adds two columns and the `day_emails` table; the owner API's body and answer grow two fields, which the app's `OwnerService` and its fakes follow.
- The owner's task titles leave the server in a mail to his own address, only after he turns it on.
- `wrangler.jsonc` triggers two crons and turns on Workers Logs; the repository-shape test pins both.
- The digest keeps its schedule; its run moves under the shared `scheduled` dispatch.

## Evidence

Cloudflare documents `ScheduledController.cron` as the expression that fired, so one `scheduled` handler dispatches several crons. D1 returns `meta.changes` for an `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, so the claim is one statement. `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with `timeZone` gives the local date and the hour and minute in workerd (ADR 0018 already relies on it for the date). Wrangler 4.167.0's configuration schema accepts `observability.logs.invocation_logs`; `@cloudflare/workers-types` 5.20261123.1 declares `headers?: Record<string, string>` on the send builder, while whether Email Service keeps `Auto-Submitted` on delivery is unproven until the first production mail is read (plan Step 22).

## Revisit when

The opted-in owners outgrow 50 sends per tick across the catch-up window, the owner wants a retry, a choice of days, or the mail at another address, or Gmail's bulk-sender rules start to apply (one-click unsubscribe).
